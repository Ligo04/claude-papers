A moving roll, a flexible strip, and surfaces that attach and detach.

Overview

Applying adhesive tape to secure wire harnesses or seal packages requires robots to coordinate a flexible strip, a moving roll, and surfaces that attach and detach. Resolving every wound layer is computationally expensive and can suppress roll motion at practical solver tolerances. TapeSim represents most still-wound material as a rigid cluster, while an advancing deformable collar enables progressive dispensing. The released strip remains flexible and can adhere to new surfaces. Releasable bonds simplify the internal adhesive interfaces.

Controlled swing tests evaluate roll motion and computational cost. At 32 turns, clustering gives 3.2–3.4× mean physics-step speedups at a fixed Newton tolerance and 4.5–8.4× for comparable roll motion. Across five real-motion Stick replays, the clustered variants reduce mean image-plane roll-landmark error by 23–29% relative to the fully deformable cohesive baseline. Paired real/simulation policy evaluations cover Peel, Stick, and Wrap, and a teleoperated box-sealing demonstration combines attachment, dispensing, cutting, and sealing.

Method

TapeSim concentrates deformation near the release front and along the free strip. The still-wound interior moves as a rigid cluster, while a short deformable collar advances as tape is dispensed.

Releasable interface bonds

Cohesive contact couples a non-penetration barrier with friction and adhesion. For a selected adhesive interface, a virtual tetrahedron replaces these terms with one shape-preserving bond energy, improving conditioning without adding material or degrees of freedom.

Bonds form between nearby eligible surfaces and release when the opening force exceeds a threshold. Compression alone does not trigger release.
